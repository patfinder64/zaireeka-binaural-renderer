Zaireeka Binaural Renderer

A browser-based binaural rendering tool for the Flaming Lips' 1997 experimental album, "Zaireeka".

Each of its eight songs consists of four sets of stereo tracks, one from each disc. While designed to be played simultaneously on four separate audio systems, this tool utilizes audio spatialisation for headphones (ASH) and binaural room impulse response (BRIR) data to simulate the full experience on a single pair of headphones.

Once you load up all the necessary files and choose an output directory, you can select from the available BRIR room data and then place each disc's audio tracks at one of the available "azimuths" (the horizontal angle from a reference cardinal direction, in this case, the direction you're facing). After the settings are in place and you hit "Generate", all four discs are rendered together, each channel placed as a virtual speaker with an ASH BRIR, and mixed to one headphone track.

The tool is designed to auto-sync all four tracks, but if you want to simulate the tracks being slightly out-of-sync, you can add an audio delay to any of the discs. You can also choose to either render the entire album or just a single track, normalize the audio (either track-by-track or for the album as a whole), or adjust the volume of any particular disc. This way, in keeping with the original spirit of the album, no single generated output is a "definitive" version of "Zaireeka" and you're encouraged to experiment with different rooms and speaker layouts to develop your own unique versions of the album.

This tool expects that you own and have downloaded a digital copy of the album (preferably in .flac format) and a BRIR dataset for ASH. Everything runs locally in the browser, no need to install any third-party applications or extensions.

WARNING: "This recording also contains frequencies not normally heard on commercial recordings and on rare occasion has caused the listener to become disoriented."

Music folder The album folder that contains Disc 1 … Disc 4 (FLAC, WAV, MP3, AAC/M4A or Ogg/Opus).

Don't have the album? Buy the official 4-disc digital release (24-bit/44.1 kHz, DRM-free) on Qobuz. Download it, unzip it, then choose the album's folder above.

BRIR folder The BRIRs folder of the ASH Listening Set or ASH-IR-Dataset (or a single room folder).

Don't have the BRIRs? Download the free ASH Listening Set (room list), unzip it, then choose its BRIRs folder above. It is the newer dataset: 35 rooms, several dummy heads and speaker distances, and heights above/below ear level in two rooms. The older ASH-IR-Dataset works too.

Output folder Not chosen: files go to your Downloads folder.

Direct: level of each disc's direct sound (first 2.5 ms of the BRIR) vs. the room reverb (+ = closer/clearer, − = further away), keeping its position. Delay: shifts a disc in time (negative = earlier). Height: places the disc above or below ear level (only some rooms were measured at several heights).

Output

Peak level: the loudest moment of the album (or track) is set to this level; −1 dBFS leaves headroom for later conversion to MP3/AAC. 16-bit output is dithered. FLAC files get full tags and cover art; WAV files get basic tags only.
